# Keys and unsealing

## How keys are held

- Each value is encrypted on its own (AES-GCM) with a **data key** belonging to its vault.
- Data keys are stored only **wrapped** by a **master key**.
- The master key lives in the **key service** (OpenBao, bundled) or your **HSM**, and never leaves it.
- The database alone can decrypt nothing.

## Key shares

`rv keys init` initialises the key service with **five shares, any three of which unseal it** (Shamir's secret sharing).

<Steps>

### Choose five key holders

People who won't all be unavailable at once, ideally in different teams.

### Create the shares

```sh
./rv keys init
```

Each share is printed **once**. Give each holder their share in person or through separate secure channels.

### Store them apart

Each holder keeps their share safe, away from the others: a password manager, a sealed envelope in a safe.

</Steps>

<Callout type="error">
If three or more shares are lost, **nothing in the vault can ever be decrypted again**. Nobody, including Onshore, can recover them.
</Callout>

## Unsealing after a restart

After a restart, the key service is **sealed**: every request that needs a key fails closed with `key_service_unavailable`, and the console shows the key service as unavailable.

```sh
./rv keys status    # sealed or unsealed
./rv keys unseal    # each of three key holders runs this and enters their share
```

## Rotation

In the console, under [Encryption keys](/guides/encryption-keys/): rotate the master key (re-wraps every data key), rotate a vault's data key (new writes use it; optionally re-encrypt stored values), and set a schedule of 30, 60, 180 or 360 days.

## Lookups without decrypting

Searchable fields use a **blind index**: a keyed hash of the value, so a value can be found without decrypting anything.
