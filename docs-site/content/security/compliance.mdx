# Compliance notes

<Callout type="warning">
Nothing here is legal advice. Onshore provides technical controls and evidence; it doesn't by itself make any organisation compliant with any law.
</Callout>

## What Onshore gives you

| Obligation | How Onshore helps |
| --- | --- |
| Keep certain data in the country | Values stored only in a vault in that country; tokens travel instead; the residency map shows every flow's status from evidence |
| Conditions on transfers abroad | Tokenization or cloaking before data leaves; a recorded **legal ground** per connection, shown in reports |
| Know where data is | The residency map, with seen and declared flows, exported for governance tools |
| Limit who sees data | Access rules, masking, purpose, approved networks, reveal limits |
| Prove who saw what | Hash-chained audit log, signed reports, per-person access timelines |
| Data subject requests | Export, erase and restrict one person's data, each recorded |
| Records of processing, registers | Reports and exports to start from |

## The central legal question

Whether tokens or cloaked text count as personal data, and whether sending them abroad is a transfer, depends on the law and on whether the recipient could re-identify people. **No regulator guidance reviewed says that tokens are outside any regulation.** Record your counsel's view as each connection's legal ground and as your scope definition.
