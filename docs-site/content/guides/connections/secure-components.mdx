# Secure components

Every system an ID number passes through has to be defended. **Secure components** are forms and reveal boxes that sit in a frame served by your own vault. The customer types into the frame; your page and your servers get a token back, and never see the value.

## Turn them on

<Steps>

### List the allowed sites

In **Vaults**, open **Secure components** and list the exact sites allowed to show the frame, one per line, such as `https://app.yourbank.example`. Mobile apps use `rvapp://` and their app ID, such as `rvapp://com.example.wallet`.

<Shot src="/screenshots/vaults-components.png" alt="Vaults, with the Secure components section" />

With no list, components are off. Wildcards are refused. `http://localhost` is allowed on test vaults only. Select **Save sites**.

### Allow the action

The service account your server uses needs an access rule allowing `tokenize` (to collect) or `detokenize` (to reveal).

### Add the code

Your server mints a single-use **client token**, and your page mounts the form. See [Collect and reveal in the front end](/developers/recipes/collect-and-reveal/), and the [web](/developers/sdks/web/), [iOS](/developers/sdks/ios/), [Android](/developers/sdks/android/) and [React Native](/developers/sdks/react-native/) references.

</Steps>

## What protects the value

- The frame is a **different origin**, so your page's scripts can't read what's typed or shown, even if your page is compromised.
- Client tokens are **single use**, last 2 minutes by default (10 at most), and are tied to one site and named fields.
- Access rules are checked again when the token is used. Removing a site or suspending a service account stops tokens already issued.
- A typo shows inside the frame and doesn't use the token up, so the person can correct it.
- A revealed value hides itself after a time you choose (30 seconds by default, up to 300).

<Callout type="warning">
**You decide who may ask for a reveal.** The vault doesn't know your end users: your server must check that this person may see this record before it mints a token.
</Callout>

## Not protected against

- A stolen reveal token used outside a browser. Bind the token to the person's IP address where you can.
- A compromised page drawing a fake form beside the real one.
- Malware on the person's device.
- On mobile, a hostile library in your own app: apps have no boundary like a browser frame. See the [iOS](/developers/sdks/ios/), [Android](/developers/sdks/android/) and [React Native](/developers/sdks/react-native/) SDKs.
